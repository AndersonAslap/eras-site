1350

Realistic · Rural France, 1350 · EXAMPLE

Old Joe and the rest that never came

“He worked his whole life, raised 6 daughters and, just when he could finally rest, the bubonic plague sent the bill.”

Read the whole story

You were born around 1280 in a village of mud houses and spent your life reading the sky better than any priest. You raised your 6 daughters, who married and moved far away — one went all the way to Paris, which in the village was like going to the Moon. After working the fields your whole life, you could finally rest, but only after spending your last days slowly dying of the bubonic plague, which arrived with a merchant who swore he just had a cold.

Real fact

  • The Black Death reached France in 1347–1348 and killed between a third and half of Europe’s population.
  • Many people believed the disease spread through “bad air”.
79

Fantastic · Pompeii, Roman Empire, 79 · EXAMPLE

Julia, the baker with a front-row seat to Vesuvius

“She fled Vesuvius with a basket of bread and opened a branch in Naples.”

Read the whole story

Daughter of a freedman who made his fortune selling bread to gladiators, you turned the family oven into the town’s official gossip spot. When Vesuvius started smoking, you thought it was the neighbor’s oven. In fate’s fantastic mode, you jumped on a fishing boat with a basket of loaves and opened history’s first refugee bakery.

Real fact

  • Vesuvius buried Pompeii and Herculaneum in AD 79.
  • Archaeologists found carbonized loaves divided into eight slices.
1889

Fantastic · Paris, France, 1889 · EXAMPLE

Lulu and the tower nobody wanted

“She defended the Eiffel Tower when everyone hated it — and spent her life saying “I told you so”.”

Read the whole story

A seamstress at a brand-new department store, you learned that a good hat solves 80% of anyone’s problems. While half of Paris signed protests against “that hideous iron tower”, you climbed to the second floor with a basket of croissants and declared the view the most beautiful thing in the world. The photo became a postcard.

Real fact

  • The Eiffel Tower opened for the 1889 World’s Fair.
  • Artists published a protest against the tower in 1887.
1780

Realistic · Edo (Tokyo), Japan, 1780 · EXAMPLE

The carver who knew every wave in Edo

“He saved the woodblocks before his clothes and started again the next day, in borrowed pajamas.”

Read the whole story

An apprentice in a woodblock workshop at 12, you spent three years just sharpening tools before carving a geisha’s first strand of hair. You became one of the best carvers in town, but the name on the print was always the painter’s. When one of Edo’s famous fires hit the neighborhood, you saved the woodblocks before your own clothes.

Real fact

  • Ukiyo-e prints were made by a team: painter, carver, printer and publisher.
  • Fires were so common they were called “the flowers of Edo”.
1324

Fantastic · Timbuktu, Mali Empire, 1324 · EXAMPLE

The caravan driver who saw the richest king in the world go by

“He traded salt for gold all his life and became famous for a single story: the day Mansa Musa passed by.”

Read the whole story

You led camels loaded with slabs of salt across the Sahara and knew the price of everything — except patience. In 1324 you saw Mansa Musa’s caravan leave for Mecca carrying so much gold that, they say, prices in Cairo were never the same. In fantastic mode, the king himself gave you a nugget and you spent the rest of your life telling the story in every market.

Real fact

  • Mansa Musa’s pilgrimage to Mecca (1324) was so lavish it affected the price of gold in Cairo.
  • In Mali, salt from the Sahara was traded for gold.
1875

Realistic · Dodge City, Kansas, 1875 · EXAMPLE

The trail cook who never fired a shot

“He crossed the Wild West without a single duel — the most dangerous weapon was his beans.”

Read the whole story

You cooked for twenty cowboys from a chuck wagon on the cattle drive from Texas to Kansas. The outfit had Black, Mexican and white cowboys, and they all agreed on one thing: your coffee could melt a horseshoe. You reached Dodge City with everyone alive, no duels and a bean recipe that became a legend.

Real fact

  • The great cattle drives took herds from Texas to the Kansas railheads.
  • About one in four cowboys was Black.
1000

Realistic · Iceland, 1000 · EXAMPLE

Gudrun and the longest winter of the saga

“She never had a vote at the assembly — women didn’t — but she never lost an argument.”

Read the whole story

A farmer on a frozen coast, you raised sheep, made cheese and settled neighbors’ feuds better than any judge. When the Althing assembly debated whether the island would become Christian, you had no vote — but you brought cheese to those who did and gave your opinion for three days straight from the family booth. The family saga says nobody ever beat you in an argument.

Real fact

  • The Althing, Iceland’s assembly, was founded around 930.
  • Around the year 1000, the assembly decided to adopt Christianity.
