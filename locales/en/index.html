Remember the “who would you be” quizzes?

They were fun for five minutes. Then you found out there were only half a dozen answers — and half your friends were also “the wise wizard”.

On Eras, the result is yours and nobody else’s.

  1. YouYour age, your personality, your ancestry (if you want to share it).
  2. Any eraFrom the pharaohs’ Egypt to jazz-age New York — or any year you type.
  3. Any placeA country, a village, a city that no longer exists.
  4. A one-of-a-kind lifeWritten on the spot, with what was likely for someone like you, right there.

Everything a life can hold (and your phone too)

Eras does the historian’s heavy lifting. You just choose and have fun.

  • Three questions, done

    Who you are, when and where, and the tone. No bank-style sign-up form.

    Over twenty curated periods with historical context — or any year and place you can imagine.

    What was truly likely, or the version where you escape Vesuvius with a basket of bread.

    Every story comes with what really happened back then. You learn without noticing.

Same person, two fates

Same age, same city, same year. Only the level of realism changes.

Anne, 19, Manchester, 1830 EXAMPLE

Realistic

Anne and the six o’clock whistle

You started at the cotton mill at 11, like half the girls on your street. By 19 you could piece a broken thread with your eyes closed and sang loudly to beat the noise of the machines — the overlooker pretended not to hear. On Sundays you saved a penny a week in a tea tin, to one day open a hat shop.

“Twelve hours a day at the mill, a penny a week in the tin and a dream of hats.”

Fantastic

Anne, inventor of the whistle

Tired of being late, you took a clock apart and built a steam alarm that woke up the whole street. The mill owner tried to buy the invention; you sold it to his rival, opened the hat shop and became the first woman in Manchester with an “inventor” sign on her door.

“She invented a steam alarm and traded the mill for a hat shop.”

Questions everybody asks

Is it really free?

Yes. Eras is free, with a daily story limit (currently 5 a day [TO CONFIRM]) to keep AI costs under control. We don’t ask for a credit card.

Why do I need an account?

To keep your stories, apply each person’s daily limit and let you delete everything whenever you want. An e-mail and a password are enough.

What do you do with my data, like ethnicity?

Ethnicity or ancestry is optional and only used, with your consent, to write the story. It never appears on the public link and is deleted with the story or your account. Details on the privacy page.

Is it historically accurate?

The AI uses real historical context (events, common jobs, life expectancy) and every story includes the “real facts behind your story”. The story itself is fiction — especially in fantastic mode. Think of a good history book with a sense of humor, not an exam.

What if the result is offensive?

The tone is always light, but the humor never targets the suffering of oppressed groups, and there is no graphic violence or sexual content. Texts go through moderation, and anyone can report a shared story.

How long does it take?

Usually less than a minute for the story with its image. Meanwhile the app shows your “time travel” happening.

Can I delete my stories?

Yes. Deleting a story takes its link down immediately, and deleting your account erases all your data and images.

Can I pick any year and place?

Yes: pick an era from the catalog or type any year (back to 3000 BC) and any place. With a catalog era, the story gets curated historical context.

Which era would you live in?

It takes less time than a quiz. And the result is yours alone.
